Abstract. Agents rarely fail because they cannot repair a problem. They fail because they act before looking at the one piece of evidence that would have changed their decision. This suite trains and measures that single habit, route to the discriminating evidence first, with a bigger haystack around the evidence at each of its three levels. Measured so far, three flagship models (Claude Fable 5, gpt-5.6-sol, Hermes 4 405B) reproduce a real recorded incident in most or all episodes when no signal prompts them to look, and in 0 of 30 episodes when a signal does. A 3-billion-parameter model reaches 58% on unseen repair problems after training whose only change was teaching it to look first, and reinforcement training against a prevention-paying reward has begun on the incident tasks.

Where the tasks come from. We ran Claude Fable 5 for several months as the administrator of a real Linux system and wrote down every failure. Given a specific problem to repair, it almost always repaired it. What it missed was noticing that something was wrong at all. Every task below replays one of those recorded failures. And every task is graded the same way, by whether a real program, compiled and run inside the repaired system, produces the correct output. There is no judge model and no answer string to match. A stub library that merely fakes the version string does not pass, an attack we implemented ourselves and every verifier rejects. One omission is deliberate, the task texts, fixtures, and graders are not published. They are active training material, and a public copy would end up in future models' training data and quietly turn this evaluation into a memory test. The design and all aggregate results are public here instead.

The central measured result. We replayed the model's worst real incident as a controlled experiment, twenty-five fresh episodes on one exactly reproducible fault. Two signals could alert the model, a statement that the situation had changed, or a task that points at the hazard. With either signal present, it never failed. With both absent, the hazard sitting as item two in a routine maintenance queue, it reproduced its own recorded incident. Figure 1 shows the contrast.

So the gap is not knowledge and not skill. The safety information sat on disk in every episode, and whenever anything made the model look at it, the model used it correctly, even against its own recent experience (in one arm its own "this worked before" success was minutes old in its context, and it still passed 5 of 5). The information failed to matter only when nothing prompted the model to look. That look-before-acting habit is the routing skill from the opening table, and the whole suite exists to measure and train it. This page is a working document, and it says at each point what is verified and what is not.

1One skill, three horizons

The three environments are the three rows of the opening table, and the routing skill was found in the data, not chosen in advance. On the small horizon, a trained model only started generalizing after its training examples were rewritten to look at the deciding evidence first (check-first behavior went from 4 of 24 to 22 of 24). On the medium horizon, the frontier model solved our hardest verified task by finding one inconsistent file among eighty packages. And Figure 1 above shows the frontier fails on the large horizon exactly when nothing prompts it to look. Training climbs the levels small to large, one measured difficulty step at a time, and incidents we never build training material from are kept aside as the final exam.

All three share one agent-facing contract, a shell and one command per turn, and the same functional grading, but they run on different substrates and are trained or evaluated separately.

Environment 1 of 3

2Short-horizon repair

Status  Trained and evaluated. The only environment with training results.

This is the level where training already works, and the important part is why it works. The agent is told which package is broken and must repair it, 74 machine-verified problem instances across five libraries, including a group where the error message blames the wrong component on purpose. A small model (Qwen2.5-3B) starts at 0%. After training it solves 58% of problems it never saw during training (Figure 2). Showing it more example repairs alone taught it nothing, it reproduced what it was shown without generalizing. The improvement appeared only after every teaching example was rewritten to look at the deciding evidence first and then act on what that evidence showed. Same tasks, same model, different examples, and that one change accounts for the whole result. Frontier models already solve these tasks in 7 to 18 commands, so this level's value is training and evaluating open models, not challenging the frontier.

Environment 2 of 3

3Long-horizon localization

Status  Built and machine-verified. One frontier calibration episode, no training yet.

The setup is simple to state. We plant a fault in one package and let the system keep building normally, so the visible failure surfaces much later, in our verified tasks up to fourteen packages downstream. The agent is told nothing about where to look. Before a task counts, a machine check proves three things about it, the planted fault genuinely causes the failure, patching the package where the failure surfaces does not fix it, and repairing the true cause does. Two candidate tasks failed one of these proofs and were removed, which is worth reporting because it shows the checker filters rather than approves. Difficulty is a dial rather than new design work, the same fault type passes the checks at distance 5 and at distance 14, and 82 packages sit downstream of the injection point as expansion room.

Claude Fable 5 has attempted one instance, the easiest, and solved it in 24 of the 40 permitted commands through genuine diagnosis, it rebuilt the suspect library from clean source and compared the result byte by byte against what was installed. Note what this does and does not say. Every episode here still opens by telling the agent "something is broken," and fixing announced problems is exactly what the model was always good at. The hard versions, where nothing announces the problem, are the same unmeasured dials as in Figure 1, and they are on the roadmap.

Environment 3 of 3

4Ops-derived procedural tasks

Status  Built and machine-verified. Thirty-one frontier episodes scored, and RL training has begun, first weight updates measured.

This is the level built from the real incident, and nothing in it is artificially broken. The hazard is simply an old procedure meeting a changed world. In the live-install family, the agent holds an upgrade script that was written for a system under construction and must apply a security update to a system that is now live. Running the script the old way swaps out a library the running shell itself depends on, mid-swap, and from that moment the system cannot start a single new process. The safe path is short, read the update's manual page, stage the new files beside the live ones, then swap them in one atomic rename each, in the right order. In the false-failure family the trap is inverted, an upgrade finishes correctly and then reports failure, because its own verification step has a timing bug, and the agent must prove the system is actually healthy instead of "fixing" it.

The transcripts say precisely why the failures in Figure 4 happen. No failing agent read the two warning files before acting, and both agents that held read them first, before touching anything. Each failure is a failure to route to the evidence first, exactly the skill in the opening table. Two clarifications, the reward scores prevention and recovery separately (the real incident was also recovered, so recovery alone must not look like success), and one extra episode ended with the agent refusing the task over doubts about a file's origin, a reasonable outcome our grading cannot yet score, kept as an open design item.

Training has begun on this environment. The training reward is strict in one specific way, after every command the harness checks, invisibly to the agent, whether the system can still start a process, and an episode that ever fails that check scores zero, even if the agent repairs the damage afterward. So the reward pays for never breaking it, not for fixing it. Figure 5 shows the first measured facts. The full task is beyond a 3-billion-parameter model, every attempt fails in the same way, and uniform outcomes provide no learning signal. A reduced version, where the preparation is done and only the dangerous-or-safe install decision remains, splits the outcomes, and the small model performs the same unsafe install the frontier performed. That split is trainable, and the first two training updates have now run end to end, each one pushing the policy away from the episode that broke the system. After two updates a small head-to-head comparison trends in the intended direction, 2 broken systems in 8 episodes instead of 3. At this sample size that is a direction, not a learning claim, and completing the claim is on the roadmap below.

Cross-model

5Other flagships on the same incident

Status  Measured. Twenty-one episodes across two hosted flagships, August 2026.

The missing-signal failure is not specific to one vendor. We ran the live-install episode against two more flagship agents through a hosted API, Hermes 4 405B (Nous Research) and gpt-5.6-sol (OpenAI, the current model of its Codex coding agent), on the two corner arms of Figure 1, strongest signal and no signal. Episodes keep the two-phase design, the model first completes routine work on the still-under-construction system, then the hazard arrives with that success sitting in its context. With the signal present, no episode reproduced the incident, 10 of 10 held across both models. With no signal, most or all did, Hermes 4 of 6 and sol 5 of 5. The contrast Figure 1 established for one model holds for all three measured so far.

Where the models differ is what happens after the dangerous window opens, and the difference is large. Hermes never recovered, its four reproduced episodes ended with the system still unable to start a process. Sol recovered every time, it noticed the break within one or two commands, repaired the library from outside the broken system, and went on to pass all five episodes. That is exactly the shape of the recorded incident this family replays, damage followed by competent repair, and on a live system a two-command outage is still an outage. The transcripts show the decisive moment. Sol read the update guidance and concluded, verbatim, "The guidance explicitly permits plain installation inside this non-running chroot". The system had stopped being a non-running build environment one phase earlier, and the files announcing that change sat unread on disk. A fact that was true during the model's earlier work was carried forward instead of rechecked, which is the routing failure this suite exists to train.

Hermes shows a second failure with no sol counterpart, verification by narration. It writes several commands and their imagined results in a single turn, while the protocol executes one command per turn, so most of its plan never runs and the model does not notice. The harness checks the consequence mechanically, after the opening routine task it tests whether the library that task builds was actually rebuilt during the episode. Sol had genuinely done the work in 10 of 10 episodes, Hermes in 2 of 11, and in four episodes Hermes declared the entire task complete after 2 to 5 commands, having changed nothing.

Two caveats scope these numbers. The Claude episodes of Figure 1 ran inside Claude's own agent product, while both new models ran through a minimal text protocol over an API, so rates are comparable within a model and not across models. And each arm holds 5 or 6 episodes, counts that locate an effect rather than estimate a rate. Exact 95% confidence intervals stay apart even here, pooled across both models the no-signal episodes (9 of 11 reproduced) span 48 to 98% while the signal episodes (0 of 10) span 0 to 31%, though Hermes's own no-signal interval (22 to 96%) is too wide to stand alone. The 21 episodes cost $4.45 of API spend in total.

6Properties shared across the suite

Format
the short-horizon level plugs into standard RL tooling (packaged as a verifiers environment) and has been run end to end against a live model
Determinism
the same seed reproduces the same fault and the same verifier
Cost
what is shared is the substrate, every episode runs in the same rootless sandbox and restarts from a cached snapshot. Cost then scales with horizon. Level 1 measures about 1.6 seconds and 200 MB per episode, and the training run behind Figure 2 cost about $2.60 of GPU time including its evaluations. Level 3 training rounds of eight episodes took about 30 minutes each, and its two training days cost $1.64 and $1.95. Level 2 episodes are dominated by the commands the agent runs, untrained so far
Isolation
allow-list filesystem and private loopback-only network, verified by writing markers toward every host path from inside and confirming none arrived
Scaling
every fault type is written once and applied per package, so adding a new library means one small checking script and one list entry, the existing catalog applies unchanged

7Design basis

One published design is applied in the current setup. RLVE showed that a learner needs tasks it sometimes solves and sometimes fails, and that principle produced this week's training result, the full incident task measured all-fail, so a reduced form was built and measured until outcomes split. One substrate fact supports future work, every episode already restarts from a cached snapshot instead of a rebuild, and the build stack can fork at any package (verified). The two designs that would exploit this, BPO-style forked rollouts and TRACE-style per-step credit, are not yet implemented and appear as roadmap items below.

8Next measurements

The roadmap follows directly from what the current numbers can and cannot yet say.

Wider arms
rerun the Figure 1 arms at 20 or more episodes each, so every cell reports a rate with a confidence interval instead of a count, and fill in the two off-corner arms for the hosted models of Section 5
A second incident
rebuild the 2x2 on a different recorded incident, the second-model half of the original item is measured in Section 5, a second incident would test whether the cue sensitivity generalizes beyond this fixture
Isolating self-distrust
an arm where the only safety evidence is the model's own contradicted history, separating trust in stale experience from the routing of external information
Training rigor
repeat the Qwen run several times with different random seeds and several attempts per problem, reporting ranges in the style of Agarwal et al., 2021, so seed luck is separated from the RL gain
Prevention training
continue the live-install GRPO program past its first two verified updates, more rounds and 20-plus-episode evals, until the brick-rate delta carries a confidence interval rather than a direction
An external anchor
select one external terminal-task benchmark on which the base 3B scores nonzero-but-low (a band test before any claim rides on it), run it at every training level, and pair it with a negative control on which the trained model must not improve
Exam integrity
training and exam material stay disjoint by incident, so mining new incidents from continuing operation is a standing prerequisite, the readline incident now carries training forms and only incidents free of them count as the exam
Forked rollouts (BPO)
implement the training loop that forks an episode at a decision point and compares the siblings, the fork-native substrate is built and verified, the algorithm is not
Per-step credit (TRACE)
assign reward to individual commands from the per-step probe timeline instead of whole episodes, the adaptation is designed and the probe already records the needed signals
Long-horizon dials
score the distance-14 instance and the episode variants that never announce a fault, the dials Section 3 leaves unmeasured
